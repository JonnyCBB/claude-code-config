# Sanity check: managed print supplier comparison

**Bottom line: I wouldn't send this to the board as it stands.** The recommendation ("Vantage is cheapest") isn't supported by the paper's own numbers. Vantage looks cheapest mainly because its price includes less than the others, and once you account for that, the ranking probably inverts. There are also two governance problems the board is likely to pick up on immediately.

Caveat up front: I only have this document. I can't see the underlying quotes, so I can't verify any of the three figures are what the suppliers actually said. With S. Okafor gone, that provenance gap is itself a risk — see the last section.

---

## 1. The three prices don't cover the same scope (this is the big one)

Reading the notes against the table, here's what each price actually buys:

|                               | Vantage £68,400            | Norbury £71,200 | Castleford £79,950 |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------- | --------------- | ------------------ |
| Hardware                      | Yes                        | Yes             | Yes                |
| Servicing                     | "standard"                 | Yes             | Full managed       |
| Consumables (toner/drums)     | **No** — billed at usage   | Yes             | Yes                |
| Installation, 3 sites         | **No** — quoted separately | Yes             | Yes                |
| Secure-disposal certification | **Not mentioned**          | Yes             | Yes                |
| 4-hour on-site engineer       | No                         | No              | Yes                |

So the table ranks three different products by price and calls the smallest one the winner. Vantage isn't cheaper on a like-for-like basis; it's just a narrower package.

**The break-even test.** Norbury is £2,800 more than Vantage (+4.1%). For Vantage to genuinely be the cheaper of the two, everything Vantage excludes — consumables, installation across three sites, and disposal certification — has to come in under £2,800 in total. Across 40 devices over three years that's:

- £70 per device for the whole 3-year term
- £23 per device per year
- **under £2 per device per month**

Toner and drums alone for a multifunction device don't come in under £2 a month. Neither does installing 40 machines across three sites. On the paper's own figures, Norbury is very likely the cheaper option, and the comparison says the opposite.

**The related problem: £68,400 isn't a total.** "Consumables billed at usage" means Vantage's number is a floor with an uncapped variable cost sitting on top of it. The other two are inclusive. If the board approves this, they're approving an open-ended commitment while being told it's a fixed cheapest price. That's the sentence I'd most want to fix before this goes anywhere.

**And a possible compliance issue.** The paper describes secure-disposal certification as _mandatory_. Norbury and Castleford include it; Vantage isn't recorded as including it. If it's genuinely mandatory, Vantage's offer as quoted may not meet the requirement at all — that's not just a missing line item, it's a question of whether the recommended bid is compliant.

## 2. Castleford's number isn't a quote, and the ranking doesn't survive it

Castleford is scaled from published list price because they wouldn't quote without a site survey. Two problems:

- **The direction of error is one-way.** List price with no volume discount applied is close to a worst case. The whole point of tendering 40 units on a 3-year term is to get below list. So this isn't a symmetrical estimate — it's an overstatement of unknown size.
- **The "within 5%" claim is asserted, not derived.** There's nothing behind it. And it doesn't take much to flip the result: **a 14.5% discount off list would make Castleford the cheapest of the three** on headline price. A ~11% discount would put them ahead of Norbury. Discounts of that order are entirely ordinary at this volume and term.

So Castleford's third place is an artefact of the estimating method, not a finding. The honest version is "Castleford unpriced — refused to quote without a site survey", not a rank.

## 3. Half the approved field was never approached

Procurement's framework lists six approved suppliers. Three were contacted. The paper says so plainly, which is to Okafor's credit, but it means the board is being asked to approve "the cheapest" on the basis of 50% of the permitted field.

Compounding it, the three that _were_ approached were selected as "the three we used last time", and the recommendation's supporting reason is "we've worked with them before". That's incumbency, not a selection rationale, and a board or auditor will read it that way. If the framework carries any competition requirement, I'd check whether a 3-of-6 exercise satisfies it before this goes forward.

## 4. The arithmetic doesn't reconcile

Vantage and Norbury check out (£1,710 × 40 = £68,400; £1,780 × 40 = £71,200). Castleford doesn't: **£1,999 × 40 = £79,960, but the table says £79,950.** It's only £10 and it doesn't change anything, but a board paper where the numbers don't tie invites doubt about the ones that do.

## 5. Things a board paper on managed print should have and this doesn't

- **Click charges / cost per page.** There isn't a single one in here. On managed print this is usually the largest cost driver over a 3-year term, and mono vs colour rates vary hugely between suppliers. A comparison without them can't establish total cost, whatever the device pricing says.
- **Device specification.** Nothing confirms the 40 devices are comparable across the three quotes — speed, colour capability, finishing, duty cycle. Per-device pricing is meaningless if the devices differ.
- **VAT treatment.** Not stated. At this order of magnitude it matters for the approval figure.
- **Quote dates and validity.** No dates anywhere. Quotes expire, and we don't know how old these are.
- **Annual price escalation.** RPI/CPI uplift clauses are standard in 3-year print contracts and can move the totals materially. Not mentioned.
- **SLA comparison.** "Standard servicing" is undefined for Vantage and Norbury. Castleford's 4-hour on-site response is the only concrete commitment, and it's being penalised as cost rather than counted as value. For 40 devices across three sites, response time is a real operational risk.
- **Exit and renewal terms**, and any volume commitments or minimum charges.

---

## What I'd do before this goes to the board

1. **Get Vantage to requote inclusive** — consumables, installation, disposal certification — so there's an apples-to-apples number. I'd expect this alone to change the recommendation.
2. **Get click rates from all three**, mono and colour, and model total cost against actual print volumes.
3. **Get a firm Castleford quote.** Book the site survey; their current ranking isn't real.
4. **Approach the other three framework suppliers**, or get an explicit, documented decision from procurement that a 3-of-6 exercise is acceptable and why.
5. **Confirm the device specifications match** across all quotes.
6. **Reconstruct the provenance** — find the original quote emails/documents in Okafor's files while they're still accessible, with dates and named contacts. Right now nothing in this paper is traceable to a source, and the person who produced it has left.
7. Fix the £79,950/£79,960 discrepancy and state the VAT basis.

If there's time pressure and you need something in front of the board now, I'd reframe it as an interim update — "three of six suppliers approached, quotes not yet on a comparable basis, no recommendation yet" — rather than a recommendation to proceed. Approving Vantage on this evidence risks committing to an open-ended consumables bill, a possibly non-compliant disposal position, and a supplier that may well not be the cheapest.

None of this is a knock on the work — the paper is unusually honest about its own gaps, which is what made it possible to spot these. It just hasn't been finished.
